Methodology and input data

Equations, limitations and sources
Q = max(RQD,10)/Jn × Jr/Ja × Jw/SRF
Crown De = max(B,h)/ESR; wall De = h/ESR
P = πB/2 + 2h; supported area = P × section length
Bolts per row = ceil(P/spacing); rows = ceil(length/spacing)+1
Installed volume = area × thickness/1000
Supplied volume = installed volume/(1−rebound/100)
Mesh = area × 1.10 (if mesh is selected)
Cost = bolt metres × rate + supplied m³ × rate + mesh m² × rate

Q and De are calculated automatically. An engineer enters spacing, bolt length and Sfr thickness after checking the continuous chart. Chart boundaries are not digitised and support categories are not assigned automatically. Uniform support is assumed for the crown and both walls, with no wall Q adjustment. If h > B, wall height also governs crown De. For category C and Q ≤ 0.1, ESR=1 is applied.

Bolt quantities are a preliminary take-off: whole bolts in uniform perimeter rows, including two end rows. Corner detailing, drilling access and local reinforcement require project design. Shotcrete is a uniform thin layer measured by surface area, without corrections for curvature, roughness or overbreak filling. Mesh includes an illustrative 10% overlap allowance; rebound is a fraction of supplied mix. Rates are illustrative and exclude mobilisation, excavation, drainage, grouting, frames/RRS, taxes and other project works.

Q < 1, flagged challenging conditions or values outside this tool's assessment range (Q 0.001…1000; De 1…100) stop preliminary selection. Quantities remain visible for planning. Q is not a factor of safety. Separate block, load and bolt checks are required; this tool does not produce an approved support design.

Source: NGI, Using the Q-system, April 2025: section 2 (Q), 3.2 and Table 3-1 (RQD), 4.1 and Table 4-1 (dimensions and ESR), 4.2 and Figure 4-1 (chart and bolt length), 4.2.2 (walls), 1.4 and 4.7 (limitations). PDF page numbers are three higher than printed page numbers.
